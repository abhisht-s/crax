Final Product, Architecture, Infrastructure & Technical Decisions

⸻

1. Executive Summary

Craxii is an AI agent with its own computer. Its purpose is to allow a person to delegate work to an AI agent that independently operates a persistent computing environment, executes commands, manipulates files, works with repositories, uses development tools, accesses the internet, and maintains its own execution history. The user interacts with Craxii through a messaging interface rather than needing to operate or maintain the agent’s development environment themselves.

Craxii V0.0.1 will be our first fully self-service, commercially sellable prototype. A customer must be able to visit our website, create an account, start a seven-day free trial, connect Telegram, and automatically receive their own dedicated Craxii computer without any intervention from us. They can then message their agent, assign tasks, receive results, and return later to find their files, conversations, and persistent agent state intact. After the trial, the customer can subscribe for $49 per month to continue using Craxii.

The system will have three architectural components: the customer-facing interfaces, the Craxii Universal Backend, and the universe of individual Craxii machines. The Universal Backend is responsible for accounts, authentication, subscriptions, Telegram communication, machine provisioning, secure machine communication, lifecycle management, usage accounting, and operational reliability. Each customer’s actual AI agent runs separately inside its own dedicated AWS EC2 instance.

Our infrastructure strategy is deliberately simple. AWS will host all computing infrastructure, while Microsoft Azure AI Foundry will provide LLM inference. The Universal Backend will be written in TypeScript and run inside Docker on an AWS EC2 instance. Amazon RDS PostgreSQL will maintain central state, AWS Cognito will handle customer authentication, Razorpay will handle payments, and Cloudflare will provide our public-facing DNS and edge infrastructure.

The architecture prioritizes getting a reliable product into customers’ hands quickly. We will implement fundamental security, isolation, persistence, and payment correctness from the beginning, but defer sophisticated fleet management, unnecessary cloud orchestration, advanced model routing, and other infrastructure that is not essential to the first sellable prototype.

2. Product Vision and Scope

Craxii’s central product promise is that every customer has an AI agent with its own computer. The customer should not need to manage a development environment, maintain an agent process, manually restart services, or continuously supervise execution. They should be able to communicate their intent through Telegram and let their agent use its own computing environment to perform the work.

The agent’s computer is persistent. Its files, installed tools, repositories, workspaces, saved conversations, execution records, and other durable state are retained across ordinary process restarts and machine shutdowns. A customer returning after several days should continue interacting with the same logical Craxii installation rather than starting from an empty environment.

V0.0.1 is not intended to be a feature-complete autonomous computing platform. Its purpose is to prove that customers want this experience, that Craxii can perform useful work reliably, and that we can sell the product without requiring manual onboarding for every customer.

Normal customer onboarding, payment activation, machine provisioning, Telegram communication, machine sleep, machine wake, and usage enforcement must be automatic. Exceptional infrastructure failures, catastrophic recovery, support investigations, and destructive administrative operations may initially require operator intervention.

The initial architecture should comfortably support a small paying customer base, with approximately 100 customers as the first operational planning target. It must remain technically extensible beyond that scale, but we will not introduce enterprise-scale infrastructure solely in anticipation of hypothetical future demand.

3. Overall System Architecture

Craxii V0.0.1 consists of three logically separate layers: the Client Layer, the Craxii Universal Backend, and the Craxii Machines Universe. These layers have different responsibilities, trust boundaries, and deployment lifecycles.

3.1 Client Layer

The Client Layer consists of the Craxii website and Telegram. These are two independent interfaces through which customers interact with the same product.

The website, available at craxii.com, manages customer-facing account operations. Customers use it to sign up, authenticate, activate their trial, purchase subscriptions, connect Telegram, inspect their Craxii’s activation status, view usage allowances, and manage their subscription. The website does not execute agent tasks, operate cloud infrastructure directly, or maintain authoritative customer billing records.

Telegram is the primary conversational interface. Customers communicate with Craxii through one shared Craxii Telegram bot. They can send instructions, receive progress information where supported, and receive completed results. The Telegram bot does not independently operate customer computers; it communicates with the Universal Backend, which identifies the customer and routes messages to the correct machine.

3.2 Craxii Universal Backend

The Universal Backend is the central administrative and operational authority for the product. It maintains the relationship between customer accounts, subscriptions, Telegram identities, dedicated machines, and resource entitlements.

It receives authenticated customer requests, verifies payment and subscription events, provisions new machines, controls their lifecycle, accepts incoming Telegram messages, routes work to the appropriate agent, receives completed responses, and delivers those responses back to customers.

It also manages machine identity, secure worker authentication, trial and subscription allowances, model API spending, EC2 running-time accounting, backup metadata, and the durable records necessary to recover from infrastructure failures.

The Universal Backend does not run the customer’s AI reasoning loop or execute their shell commands. Those operations remain inside the dedicated customer machine. The Universal Backend may proxy model API requests to Azure AI Foundry because it is responsible for protecting provider credentials and enforcing spending limits.

3.3 Craxii Machines Universe

The Craxii Machines Universe consists of individual AWS EC2 virtual machines provisioned for customers.

Each customer receives a dedicated Linux VM containing the Rust Craxii runtime, its own SQLite database, persistent files, workspaces, conversation records, execution history, and trusted worker identity.

Each machine operates independently of other customer machines. A customer’s Craxii must not be able to inspect another customer’s filesystem, retrieve their messages, impersonate their worker identity, or access their infrastructure resources.

The Universal Backend manages these machines through AWS APIs and an authenticated worker communication protocol. Machines initiate outbound communication with the Universal Backend rather than exposing public inbound management services.

3.4 Communication Between Layers

The website communicates with the Universal Backend through authenticated HTTPS requests. Cognito authenticates the user, while the Universal Backend enforces ownership and subscription permissions.

Telegram communicates with the Universal Backend through the shared bot integration. Incoming Telegram messages are durably admitted centrally, associated with the correct customer, and routed to their machine.

Customer machines communicate with the Universal Backend over HTTPS using individually enrolled cryptographic identities. They receive work, acknowledge durable admission, report readiness, and export completed responses.

The machines obtain model inference through a centrally authorized model API proxy that communicates with Azure AI Foundry. This preserves centralized model spending enforcement without moving the customer’s agent execution into the Universal Backend.

4. Repository Architecture

Craxii V0.0.1 will use three separate repositories.

The existing public craxii repository remains the home of the Rust agent runtime. The existing craxii_website repository remains the home of the Next.js customer-facing website. A new private repository, craxii-control-plane, will contain the Universal Backend.

This follows Option C from our read-only Codex repository architecture audit.

4.1 Public Craxii Repository

The public craxii repository contains the working V0.0.01 agent implementation and will evolve into the software installed on each customer machine.

We will preserve its existing implementations of local SQLite persistence, durable work admission, scheduling, model execution, tool execution, filesystem operations, delivery semantics, and recovery wherever they remain appropriate.

The current V0.0.01 runtime combines some responsibilities that will move into the Universal Backend, particularly its single-owner Telegram integration. Those components will need to be separated carefully when the runtime becomes a centrally managed customer worker.

We will not rewrite the existing Rust agent in TypeScript. Rust remains the language of the customer runtime.

The existing Stage 27 deployment remains a working reference. Our first V0.0.1 owner machine will be newly provisioned through the same process intended for real customers rather than modifying or adopting the historical machine.

4.2 Private Universal Backend Repository

The new craxii-control-plane repository will be private from inception.

It will contain our TypeScript/Node.js application, central PostgreSQL migrations, account and entitlement logic, payment integration, Telegram infrastructure, AWS machine orchestration, worker authentication, model proxy, usage accounting, and operational tooling.

The repository will also contain appropriate Docker configuration, deployment definitions, tests, and infrastructure integration code.

Keeping the control plane private preserves our commercial implementation details and provides separate source and deployment permissions. It is not itself a security mechanism; all runtime security requirements remain necessary regardless of repository visibility.

4.3 Website Repository

The existing craxii_website repository remains separate.

It contains our Next.js frontend and will evolve from the current marketing website into the customer-facing product portal.

Its source code and deployment lifecycle remain independent of the Universal Backend.

4.4 Cross-Repository Compatibility

The Rust worker and TypeScript control plane will communicate through a language-neutral, explicitly versioned protocol.

The current recommended contract format is JSON Schema with documented HTTP and, where appropriate, WebSocket message envelopes. The worker protocol will be distinct from the existing V0.0.01 native-client protocol.

Repository separation must not cause independent releases to become incompatible.

A customer machine may remain asleep for several weeks while the Universal Backend receives multiple updates. When that customer returns, their older worker must continue communicating with the updated backend or be safely upgraded before receiving work.

Compatibility tests must therefore cover previously released worker versions, not merely the newest Rust and TypeScript source trees.

The control plane and worker remain independently deployable.

5. Universal Backend Technology

The Universal Backend will be written in TypeScript and run on Node.js.

We considered Rust, Go, and TypeScript before selecting the final language.

Rust would provide strong performance and type guarantees, but our experience developing V0.0.01 demonstrated that its language complexity and compilation constraints can increase Codex implementation and debugging iterations.

Go would offer a simpler programming model and excellent infrastructure capabilities, but TypeScript offers a particularly mature ecosystem for the integrations our backend requires.

The Universal Backend is primarily an orchestration application. Its work involves network communication, PostgreSQL transactions, payment verification, cloud API calls, worker communication, and background operations. It does not perform computationally intensive model inference or customer tool execution.

TypeScript is well suited to this workload, and its development ergonomics align with our priority of reducing time to a sellable product.

We will use strict compiler configuration, runtime validation for untrusted inputs, carefully bounded asynchronous operations, explicit transaction boundaries, and durable PostgreSQL state rather than relying on in-memory process state for important obligations.

Node.js concurrency is sufficient for the anticipated workload. Correctness will depend on durable operations, unique constraints, idempotency, and appropriate synchronization rather than language-level concurrency features alone.

6. Universal Backend Hosting Infrastructure

The Universal Backend will run continuously in AWS Mumbai, region ap-south-1.

The initial compute instance is an Amazon EC2 t3.small with 2 vCPUs and 2 GiB RAM.

We selected this specification because the backend is primarily I/O-bound and will not execute customer AI workloads. It provides an inexpensive starting point while allowing the instance to be resized if measured demand justifies additional resources.

The backend will run inside Docker rather than directly as a native process on the EC2 operating system.

Docker provides a reproducible application environment and enables us to package the Node.js runtime, compiled application, and required production dependencies into a versioned image.

The private GitHub repository will be the source of truth for application code. GitHub Actions will build approved releases, publish Docker images to Amazon Elastic Container Registry, and support controlled deployment onto the backend EC2 instance.

The production machine will run the built image rather than independently building the full application from source.

We are deliberately not adopting Kubernetes, Amazon ECS, Fargate, or a separate container orchestration platform for V0.0.1.

The initial architecture consists of one backend EC2 instance and one logical Universal Backend service.

This does not provide full high availability. If the backend host becomes unavailable, central customer operations and message routing may be temporarily interrupted. The system must preserve durable obligations and recover safely after the backend restarts.

The instance can later be upgraded from t3.small to a larger instance without changing the application architecture.

7. Central PostgreSQL Database

Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL is the finalized central database service.

We considered self-hosting PostgreSQL inside Docker but selected RDS to reduce the operational burden of database infrastructure maintenance.

The database is a critical shared component. Losing central subscription, machine ownership, provisioning, or message records could affect every customer even if their individual machines remained healthy.

RDS provides managed PostgreSQL infrastructure, automated backup capabilities, monitoring integrations, and established recovery mechanisms.

Craxii remains responsible for its schema, migrations, query correctness, access permissions, and recovery verification.

7.1 Database Instance

The initial RDS instance will be db.t4g.small, providing 2 vCPUs and 2 GiB RAM.

It will run in AWS Mumbai using a Single-AZ configuration.

We will begin with approximately 20 GiB of general-purpose SSD storage, subject to verifying the supported RDS storage configuration during setup.

The database will be encrypted at rest, have public accessibility disabled, and maintain seven days of automated backups with point-in-time recovery.

Deletion protection will be enabled.

We selected Single-AZ because the Universal Backend itself initially runs on a single EC2 instance. Multi-AZ database availability can be introduced when end-to-end availability requirements justify the additional cost.

The database can be resized later if monitoring identifies insufficient memory, CPU capacity, storage, or connection headroom.

7.2 Central Data Ownership

PostgreSQL will store canonical customer accounts, authentication-provider associations, phone details, consent records, trial and subscription entitlements, payment events, Telegram associations, machine ownership, cloud resource identifiers, worker identities, and operational state.

It will also store durable provisioning obligations, pending work-delivery records, usage accounting, model-budget reservations, machine lifecycle state, backup inventory, and administrative audit records.

PostgreSQL will not become the canonical filesystem or execution-history database for every customer agent.

That information remains inside each customer’s persistent machine storage.

The control plane may temporarily store routed messages and exported responses where necessary for reliable delivery, but that does not change the worker’s ownership of its long-term agent history.

7.3 Database Migrations

Database schema changes will be maintained as version-controlled SQL migrations inside craxii-control-plane.

Migrations will be created and tested against local PostgreSQL before being applied to production through a controlled deployment process.

Production migrations must be reviewed for compatibility with currently deployed backend versions, especially when they modify subscription, payment, or machine ownership records.

The earlier SQLx CLI discussion applied to the previous Rust backend proposal. Since the backend is now TypeScript, the exact PostgreSQL migration tooling remains an implementation choice rather than a finalized architectural dependency.

7.4 Visual Database Administration

We will use a PostgreSQL GUI such as DBeaver when visual inspection is useful.

It can display schemas, tables, rows, cell values, and relationships, providing functionality comparable to the visual database management experience previously used through Supabase.

Production access will use an approved private connectivity path rather than making RDS publicly accessible.

8. Database and Infrastructure Security

The central RDS database must remain inaccessible from the public internet.

Its network security group will permit PostgreSQL access only from the Universal Backend’s approved security group.

Connections between the backend and RDS will use TLS with proper certificate and hostname verification.

The application will connect using restricted PostgreSQL credentials rather than the database’s master administrative account.

Database credentials and other infrastructure secrets will be stored through AWS Secrets Manager and accessed using restricted AWS IAM permissions.

No database password or production secret may be committed to GitHub, included in Docker images, or exposed to customer machines.

Storage encryption, backup encryption, deletion protection, and regular access review form part of the baseline.

The TypeScript application must use parameterized queries, validate external input, enforce customer-level authorization, and ensure that an authenticated customer cannot access another customer’s records.

A private RDS database does not protect against application-level authorization vulnerabilities. Customer ownership checks must therefore be enforced by the backend independently of network restrictions.

9. Customer Authentication

AWS Cognito Essentials is our finalized authentication provider.

We compared Clerk, AWS Cognito, and Microsoft Entra External ID. Clerk was excluded, and Cognito was selected for its managed authentication capabilities and alignment with our AWS-hosted infrastructure.

V0.0.1 will support email/password authentication and Google sign-in.

Cognito will manage customer credential verification, password recovery, authentication flows, and token issuance.

The website will use Cognito’s supported browser authentication flow, including Authorization Code with PKCE.

The Universal Backend will verify authentication tokens before allowing protected customer operations.

Cognito is the authority for authentication, but not for Craxii subscription or machine ownership.

Our own PostgreSQL customer records will associate each account with its stable Cognito identity. Email addresses will not serve as immutable customer identifiers.

Customers will also provide the required phone information and legal consent during onboarding. The exact verification and abuse-control requirements will be implemented according to the finalized onboarding policy.

Telegram identities and worker machine identities will remain separate from website authentication.

10. Website Architecture and Hosting

The Craxii website will remain a statically deployed Next.js application hosted on Cloudflare.

We decided not to introduce a separate server-side Next.js application for V0.0.1 because the Universal Backend already owns the sensitive business operations.

The browser will authenticate customers through Cognito and communicate directly with api.craxii.com for authorized account operations.

The website will provide signup, login, trial activation, Telegram linking, subscription purchase, billing management, machine status, and usage information.

It will not connect directly to PostgreSQL, hold cloud-management credentials, or perform authoritative payment verification.

Sensitive operations must be enforced server-side by the Universal Backend. Hiding a button or route in the frontend is not an authorization boundary.

This architecture allows the existing Cloudflare-hosted website to evolve into the customer portal without adding another application server.

11. Public API, Domain, and Network Ingress

The finalized backend hostname is api.craxii.com.

The existing website remains available at craxii.com.

Our public API architecture is:

Cloudflare Free → Caddy reverse proxy → Dockerized TypeScript Universal Backend.

Cloudflare already hosts the website, so we will continue using its Free plan and add the API subdomain under the existing domain.

The expected additional Cloudflare subscription cost is $0 per month, assuming we use only included features.

Caddy will act as the reverse proxy running on the backend EC2 instance. It will forward HTTPS requests to the Node.js application without exposing the application’s internal port directly to the internet.

One API hostname will initially support customer requests, Telegram integration, Razorpay webhooks, and authenticated worker communication.

These interfaces will remain logically separate and use different authentication and validation mechanisms.

The backend’s origin should be protected against unnecessary direct access that bypasses Cloudflare security controls. TLS configuration, origin restrictions, and application-level protections must be verified during deployment.

Customer workers may use long-lived authenticated connections or reconnecting HTTPS communication. We must verify that Cloudflare’s connection and timeout behavior does not interfere with normal worker operation.

We will not introduce an AWS Application Load Balancer, Cloudflare Workers, AWS Lambda, or Azure Functions for V0.0.1.

12. Customer Computing Infrastructure

All customer Craxii machines will run on AWS EC2.

This is an explicit change from our earlier AWS/Azure alternating-placement architecture.

The earlier two-cloud provisioning design is superseded. We no longer need Azure VM provisioning, Azure worker networking, cross-cloud placement, or AWS-to-Azure provisioning federation for V0.0.1.

Azure’s role is now model inference.

Each customer receives a dedicated EC2 t3.large instance running Ubuntu 24.04 LTS on x86-64 hardware.

The instance provides 2 vCPUs and 8 GiB RAM.

Each installation will have an approximately 30 GiB encrypted operating-system disk and a separate 80 GiB encrypted EBS gp3 data disk.

The data disk uses ext4 and is mounted at /srv/craxii-data.

The operating-system disk is considered rebuildable. The persistent data disk contains the customer’s canonical agent state.

The current Rust runtime’s persistent state paths, including /var/lib/craxii, /srv/craxii/workspaces, and /home/craxii, will be mapped onto the dedicated persistent disk.

This separation is essential because restarting or replacing a VM must not automatically destroy the customer’s files and agent history.

13. Customer VM Images and Automatic Provisioning

New customer machines will be created from approved, prebuilt, versioned AWS machine images.

The image will contain Ubuntu 24.04, the approved Rust Craxii runtime release, required system dependencies, the trusted service configuration, the model-controlled shell execution boundary, and the necessary AWS management agent.

We will not clone the public Craxii repository and compile the full Rust application independently every time a customer signs up.

A single verified release will instead be built and incorporated into a reusable machine image.

Cloud-init will handle narrow per-installation configuration and secure enrollment during initial startup.

13.1 Provisioning Authority

The Universal Backend will use the official AWS SDK for JavaScript v3 to create and manage customer resources.

It will authenticate to AWS using the backend EC2 instance’s restricted IAM role, not permanent AWS access keys embedded in source code or environment files.

The backend will have permission to create approved customer instances and EBS disks, attach disks, inspect state, start and stop machines, and perform authorized backup operations.

The runtime must not have unrestricted authority to modify IAM policies, create arbitrary network exposure, or delete unrelated infrastructure.

13.2 Provisioning Process

When a customer becomes eligible for a machine through trial activation or verified payment, the backend must first establish a durable provisioning obligation in PostgreSQL.

That obligation identifies the customer, installation, approved machine configuration, and intended cloud resources.

The backend then creates an encrypted persistent EBS data volume in the selected Availability Zone.

It launches an approved EC2 instance using the pinned Craxii image, t3.large instance type, correct subnet, restricted security group, approved instance profile, and required metadata protection.

The data disk is attached to the instance and identified by its actual AWS volume identity.

The guest initialization process verifies the expected disk, mounts it safely, establishes persistent application paths, and starts the trusted Craxii service.

The worker then enrolls with the Universal Backend and reports its recovered readiness.

The backend marks the installation ready only after verifying that the correct worker has authenticated and that its persistent storage and local runtime have initialized successfully.

An EC2 instance entering the running state does not mean the customer’s Craxii is ready.

13.3 Provisioning Idempotency

Provisioning must survive failures without creating duplicate customer machines.

The backend will record provisioning intent before making external cloud API calls.

It will use stable operation identifiers, AWS-supported idempotency tokens, resource identifiers, PostgreSQL uniqueness constraints, and reconciliation against actual cloud resources.

If AWS creates an instance but the backend crashes before recording the result, restarting the provisioning operation must discover and reconcile that instance rather than blindly creating another.

If the correct result cannot be established safely, the operation must stop and surface a support-required state.

The intended outcome is effectively one customer installation and one active authorized machine, not a false assumption that distributed cloud operations execute exactly once.

14. Customer Machine Networking and Isolation

Customer machines require outbound internet access so they can communicate with the Universal Backend, access development resources, install packages, and perform legitimate customer-requested work.

For V0.0.1, we accepted the audited AWS networking approach of using customer instances with public IPv4 connectivity but zero permitted inbound security-group rules.

This avoids paying for a shared AWS NAT Gateway during the initial product stage.

Customer instances will not expose public SSH or other inbound management services.

A public IPv4 address does not itself constitute inbound permission. The effective security-group rules must deny unsolicited inbound access, including connections originating from other customer machines.

The instances will initiate their connections to api.craxii.com.

Administrative access will use AWS Systems Manager Session Manager under a separately authorized operator identity.

Customer worker instances must not receive cloud permissions allowing them to create other customer machines, access central database secrets, or manage unrelated AWS resources.

The existing separation between the trusted Craxii service and the model-controlled shell remains fundamental.

The model-controlled execution environment must not be able to obtain privileged AWS instance credentials through metadata access or read trusted service credentials from the filesystem.

The initial network configuration must be validated through actual canary tests before paying customers are admitted.

⸻

15. Worker Identity, Enrollment, and Secure Communication

Worker communication will use HTTPS, Ed25519 cryptographic identities, signed authentication challenges, and short-lived sessions.

We evaluated this approach against mutual TLS with independently managed worker certificates.

We selected signed machine identities over HTTPS because they can provide the required authentication and authorization properties without introducing a private certificate-authority lifecycle for V0.0.1.

15.1 Initial Enrollment

Every newly provisioned Craxii machine will receive a unique, short-lived, one-time enrollment credential associated with its installation and provisioning operation.

The trusted worker service will generate its own Ed25519 keypair.

The private key will be protected from the model-controlled shell and stored with the worker’s trusted persistent identity state.

The worker will submit an enrollment request containing its public key and proof that it possesses the corresponding private key.

The Universal Backend will verify the enrollment credential, expected installation, current lifecycle generation, and applicable machine identity evidence.

After successful verification, the backend registers the worker’s public key as the authorized identity for that installation.

The one-time enrollment credential cannot subsequently be reused to enroll another worker.

15.2 Subsequent Authentication

A previously enrolled worker proves its identity by signing fresh backend-issued authentication challenges.

The backend verifies the signature against the registered public key.

Successful authentication establishes a short-lived session associated with that exact worker identity and installation.

Sensitive operations remain subject to installation ownership and lifecycle-generation authorization.

A valid signature from one worker never authorizes access to another customer’s messages or machine state.

15.3 Replacement and Revocation

If a customer’s machine is replaced, the backend advances the installation’s lifecycle generation.

The previous worker’s identity and sessions must cease to be authoritative.

A stale machine cannot resume requesting work or exporting results simply because it still possesses an older valid keypair.

Credential revocation and generation fencing must be enforced centrally.

15.4 Latency Requirement

Security checks must not materially degrade normal message delivery or agent response latency.

Machines should reuse established authenticated connections and sessions where appropriate.

Expensive enrollment procedures should not be repeated for every Telegram message.

The system must measure connection, authentication, and delivery latency during testing.

16. Telegram Messaging Architecture

Craxii V0.0.1 will use one shared Telegram bot.

The Universal Backend owns bot authentication, incoming message processing, customer association, and outgoing response delivery.

Individual customer machines will no longer independently operate their own Telegram pollers.

16.1 Telegram Account Linking

A customer authenticates on the website and initiates a secure Telegram linking process.

The Universal Backend issues a one-time linking mechanism associated with the authenticated customer account.

When the customer completes linking, the backend associates their numeric Telegram identity with the correct Craxii account.

Telegram usernames are not considered stable customer identities.

The system must reject ambiguous, reused, or unauthorized links.

16.2 Incoming Work

An incoming Telegram message is accepted by the central backend and durably recorded before the system depends on any customer machine being available.

The backend identifies the sender, verifies the account association, checks the customer’s entitlement, and determines which machine owns the conversation.

If the machine is sleeping, the backend requests a wake-up while preserving the accepted message.

Once the authenticated worker becomes ready, the backend delivers the instruction using a stable message identity.

The worker durably admits that instruction into its local SQLite state and acknowledges the admission.

Retries must not create duplicate canonical work.

16.3 Returning Results

The worker executes the task using its local agent runtime and saves the resulting state.

It exports the completed response to the Universal Backend using a stable export identity.

The Universal Backend durably accepts the result and handles Telegram delivery.

A response must not disappear merely because the backend restarts during delivery.

Likewise, repeated export attempts must not produce duplicate accepted results.

Telegram itself can produce ambiguous send outcomes when a request succeeds but its acknowledgement is lost. We will preserve the existing conservative delivery approach rather than blindly retrying potentially delivered messages.

17. Machine Lifecycle and Sleep/Wake

Craxii customer machines are not intended to run continuously when idle.

The finalized idle policy is 30 minutes of safe inactivity.

A machine may be stopped only when doing so will not unsafely interrupt active work.

The Universal Backend maintains the machine’s desired and observed lifecycle state.

A missed heartbeat alone does not prove that the agent is idle. The system must distinguish an actually idle machine from a worker that is executing a long-running task or has temporarily lost communication.

Before stopping a machine, the backend must coordinate with the worker so that active operations are complete or durably checkpointed where safe.

The backend then requests EC2 stop.

The customer’s persistent EBS disk remains available, while the VM’s RAM contents are lost.

When a message arrives for a sleeping machine, it is first durably recorded centrally.

The backend coalesces concurrent wake requests into one lifecycle operation and starts the EC2 instance.

The guest boots, mounts its persistent disk, restores its trusted worker identity, recovers its local SQLite state, and reconnects to the Universal Backend.

Only after authenticated readiness is established can queued work be delivered.

Wake-up latency of several tens of seconds is acceptable for a sleeping machine.

Failures must preserve queued work and report truthful states such as waking, delayed, or requiring support rather than falsely reporting readiness.

18. Model Inference Infrastructure

Microsoft Azure AI Foundry is the primary LLM inference provider for Craxii V0.0.1.

This decision supersedes the original model-provider assumptions associated with the Rust prototype.

AWS hosts the computing infrastructure. Azure provides model inference.

The intention is to use the existing $10,000 Azure startup credits for eligible model inference, extending our early product runway.

Based on the credit terms you identified, the intended coverage includes Foundry models other than the excluded Anthropic offerings. The exact model deployment and billing path must still correspond to eligible Azure consumption.

18.1 Model Selection

The specific model is not finalized.

Kimi K2.5 is one candidate.

We agreed to benchmark multiple models later, evaluating actual Craxii task completion, tool-use reliability, latency, token consumption, and cost per successful task.

The cheapest token price is not necessarily the cheapest completed task.

Model selection is intentionally not a blocker for beginning Universal Backend development.

18.2 Model API Proxy

The Universal Backend will expose a narrow authenticated model-call interface to customer workers.

The trusted backend holds the Azure Foundry credentials and validates worker authorization before making provider requests.

It enforces customer-level model spending budgets and restricts authorized request parameters.

The customer agent’s reasoning loop still runs on the customer’s EC2 machine. The backend forwards model requests and responses but does not independently plan or execute the customer’s tasks.

This avoids distributing broad model-provider credentials into potentially hostile customer execution environments.

18.3 Provider Independence

The model interface should be sufficiently independent of Azure-specific request formats that another provider can be introduced later.

We are not building a sophisticated multi-model routing engine or automated provider optimization system for V0.0.1.

The initial objective is one reliable Azure Foundry inference integration with strong spending controls.

19. Commercial Product and Subscription Pricing

Craxii V0.0.1 will initially cost $49 USD per month.

This price provides access to one dedicated Craxii computer, its persistent environment, the full agent experience, and the agreed compute and model allowances.

The subscription is monthly.

We are not introducing automatic overage billing in V0.0.1.

The customer’s model API and EC2 runtime allowances will be enforced by the Universal Backend.

When an allowance is exhausted, the system must stop accepting additional chargeable work without unexpectedly charging the customer.

The exact tax treatment and payment settlement requirements remain dependent on the final Razorpay merchant configuration and applicable tax obligations.

20. Paid Subscription Resource Allowances

A paid subscription includes 180 cumulative EC2 running hours per monthly billing period and up to $20 in model API usage during that period.

We discussed six hours per day as the intended equivalent usage level.

We subsequently agreed that the allowance should be cumulative across the billing month rather than a separate strict six-hour daily cap.

This allows customers to use more than six hours on one day and less on another without losing access prematurely.

Unused allowances do not automatically roll over into the next billing period under the current policy.

The monthly entitlement and usage counters reset when the next paid billing period becomes valid.

The compute allowance measures actual EC2 running time, not merely the duration of active Telegram conversations.

The accounting implementation must handle machine restarts, delayed cloud-state reports, backend crashes, billing boundaries, and unexpected VM behavior.

When the monthly compute allowance is exhausted, new chargeable work and ordinary wake requests are blocked until the next valid entitlement period.

Existing work must be checkpointed or brought to a safe stopping point where possible.

The same principle applies to model spending.

21. Seven-Day Free Trial

A seven-day free trial is a core V0.0.1 product feature.

We deliberately retained the trial after reconsidering an earlier paid-only recommendation.

The reason is product discovery. Craxii’s value is best understood through actual use: assigning meaningful tasks, seeing the agent operate its computer, returning to saved work, and developing confidence in the persistent agent experience.

We consider that opportunity more important than avoiding the operational complexity of trials.

21.1 Trial Duration and Allowances

The trial lasts seven calendar days.

It includes up to $5 in model API usage and 12 cumulative hours of EC2 runtime.

Customers receive the real Craxii experience, including their own machine, persistent files, Telegram communication, and agent capabilities.

The trial does not require a payment method upfront.

The trial is not automatically converted into a paid subscription.

21.2 Trial Termination

The trial ends when any one of its limits is reached first.

These limits are the seven-day duration, the $5 model budget, and the 12-hour cumulative compute allowance.

At that point, Craxii stops accepting additional chargeable work and asks the customer to subscribe.

The machine is safely stopped after any necessary state-preservation operations.

The customer’s saved data remains subject to the product’s retention policy.

A customer who decides to subscribe before the trial ends may activate their paid entitlement earlier.

21.3 Trial Conversion

Because no payment method is collected at the beginning of the trial, customers must explicitly authorize their first paid subscription.

There is no automatic charge after seven days.

A trial customer who pays should continue using the same logical Craxii installation and previously saved data.

The system should not discard the trial machine’s state and create an unrelated empty environment merely because the customer converted.

21.4 Trial Abuse Prevention

We agreed that trial abuse requires protection because every trial incurs real EC2, storage, and model costs.

The intended principle is one ordinary free trial per verified customer.

Reasonable controls may include verified contact information, account-linkage checks, signup throttling, and detection of suspicious repeated registrations.

The exact repeat-trial detection policy remains to be finalized.

We should avoid imposing identity-verification friction disproportionate to the risk, but cannot permit unlimited anonymous infrastructure consumption.

22. Payments and Billing Provider

Razorpay is the finalized payment gateway for V0.0.1.

We compared Razorpay and Stripe, including the available startup benefits.

Razorpay offers a promotional processing-fee benefit associated with the existing YC arrangement. Stripe potentially offers a larger processing-volume waiver through Microsoft for Startups, but Indian merchant eligibility and onboarding introduce uncertainty.

We decided to proceed with Razorpay instead of delaying backend development.

Stripe may be evaluated later.

22.1 Responsibility Separation

Razorpay manages payment processing, applicable recurring-payment authorization, transaction outcomes, and subscription provider events.

The Universal Backend maintains Craxii’s customer entitlement and product-access state.

It must not grant paid access merely because the frontend reports a successful checkout.

Payment outcomes must be verified through trusted Razorpay mechanisms.

Payment webhooks must be authenticated, processed idempotently, and reconciled where necessary.

22.2 Future Provider Changes

Razorpay-specific integration code should remain separated from the central subscription and entitlement domain.

This allows another payment provider to be integrated later without redesigning customer-machine ownership or resource accounting.

A future payment gateway switch may still require customers to authorize new recurring mandates. We should not assume existing subscriptions can be transferred seamlessly.

22.3 USD Pricing

The intended customer-facing subscription price is $49 USD.

Craxii is based in India and intends to serve customers internationally.

Before accepting live payments, we must verify the specific Razorpay merchant account’s support for the intended recurring USD payments, supported countries and payment methods, settlement currency, fees, and compliance requirements.

This is an account-level launch verification, not a reason to postpone the central billing architecture.

23. Subscription Lifecycle and Payment Failure Handling

The Universal Backend must maintain a reliable distinction between payment collection and entitlement to consume Craxii resources.

Billing events may arrive late, be duplicated, or be delivered out of order. Payment provider availability may also be interrupted.

Important subscription state must therefore be stored durably and updated through verified, idempotent operations.

23.1 Initial Payment

A successful initial payment creates or activates the appropriate paid entitlement.

For a customer who already has a trial installation, the existing Craxii state should be preserved.

For a customer without an installation, the entitlement creates a durable provisioning obligation.

A failed initial payment does not grant paid access.

A pending payment must not be treated as successfully collected.

23.2 Renewal

A successful monthly renewal extends the customer’s paid entitlement into the next billing period.

Monthly resource allowances reset for the newly paid period.

A failed renewal does not automatically destroy customer data or terminate their machine.

The customer retains access through any already-paid entitlement period.

After that paid period ends, no additional chargeable work is admitted unless payment is successfully restored.

We agreed not to provide additional free compute merely because Razorpay is retrying a failed renewal.

23.3 Cancellation

Customers may cancel their subscriptions.

Ordinary cancellation stops future renewal while preserving access through the current paid period.

After the paid entitlement ends, chargeable work and ordinary wake-ups stop.

An immediate cancellation or data-deletion request may require a separate user-facing action and appropriate confirmation.

The customer’s retained data must be handled according to the applicable retention and deletion policy.

23.4 Usage Exhaustion

A customer who exhausts their compute or model allowance does not automatically incur additional charges.

New chargeable work is paused.

Existing durable state is preserved.

The customer must wait for a new valid entitlement period or another explicitly approved entitlement change.

23.5 Duplicate and Delayed Events

Duplicate Razorpay events must not create duplicate subscriptions, extend entitlements twice, reset allowances twice, or cause additional VM provisioning.

Delayed and out-of-order events must not blindly overwrite newer authoritative subscription state.

The backend must reconcile ambiguous conditions against Razorpay’s actual recorded transaction and subscription status.

23.6 Refunds and Disputes

The precise published refund policy remains open.

We agreed that customers whose payment succeeds but whose Craxii cannot be activated must receive a reliable resolution path, including a refund where appropriate.

Other refund requests may initially be handled through support under a published policy.

Chargebacks and payment disputes must be distinguished from ordinary merchant-initiated refunds.

Disputed payments should not permit uncontrolled new infrastructure expenditure while their status is being resolved.

Existing customer data should not be immediately destroyed because a payment dispute occurs.

The exact refund eligibility window, dissatisfaction policy, and dispute-related entitlement behavior still require final approval.

⸻

24. Usage Accounting and Hard Limits

The Universal Backend is responsible for enforcing paid and trial resource limits.

This must be based on durable accounting rather than in-memory timers.

24.1 Compute Accounting

The backend must track EC2 running time for each customer installation.

It must account for stop/start transitions, delayed AWS observations, unexpected restarts, and backend downtime.

The system must avoid counting the same interval twice or losing usage because a lifecycle event was retried.

Compute use must be associated with the correct trial or subscription period.

We are enforcing 12 cumulative trial hours and 180 cumulative paid hours per billing month.

The ordinary 30-minute idle shutdown remains active even when the customer has unused compute allowance.

24.2 Model Accounting

The backend must enforce a $5 total model allowance for trials and a $20 monthly model allowance for paying customers.

Because model calls can have variable final costs, the system should reserve an appropriately bounded amount of budget before admitting an authorized request.

Actual usage is reconciled after the provider returns usage information.

Concurrent requests must not independently spend the same remaining budget.

When the remaining allowance is insufficient, the backend must decline additional chargeable requests rather than allow unbounded overages.

24.3 Safe Limit Enforcement

Hard usage limits must be enforced without casually corrupting work in progress.

Where possible, the worker should checkpoint execution or finish an already-authorized bounded operation before the machine stops.

We should not promise that every arbitrary shell command can be interrupted without consequences.

The runtime’s existing durable execution and recovery mechanisms should be reused to preserve correctness during resource exhaustion.

25. Persistent Customer Data

Craxii’s persistence model is fundamental to the product.

Every customer machine has a dedicated 80 GiB EBS data disk.

This disk contains the customer’s local SQLite database, conversation and execution records, working files, repositories, artifacts, and other durable agent state.

The disk is separate from the approximately 30 GiB operating-system disk.

Persistent data should remain available after normal shutdown, restart, and appropriately managed compute replacement.

The bootstrap process must identify the exact EBS volume intended for the installation rather than selecting an arbitrary disk based solely on size.

An existing persistent disk must never be automatically reformatted.

Formatting is permitted only when initialization proves that the disk is newly created, empty, and belongs to the correct installation.

A machine must fail closed if its expected persistent disk is absent, incorrectly attached, or inconsistent with its recorded identity.

⸻

26. Data Retention After Cancellation or Expiry

The agreed customer-data retention target is one year after cancellation or expiry.

We initially considered shorter retention, but subsequently settled on a one-year customer retention period.

The reason is to preserve the customer’s ability to return and recover their previous Craxii environment rather than permanently losing months of accumulated files and context shortly after cancelling.

However, retaining an 80 GiB provisioned EBS disk for every former customer could become financially expensive.

AWS charges for provisioned EBS storage even when the corresponding EC2 instance is stopped.

26.1 Proposed Inactive Storage Architecture

The current preferred approach is to preserve inactive customer data using encrypted EBS snapshots rather than retaining full provisioned data volumes throughout the retention period.

Once the customer’s paid entitlement has ended, the backend would ensure the worker is safely stopped and its persistent data is in a recoverable state.

A final encrypted snapshot of the customer’s canonical data disk would then be created.

After verifying the snapshot and recording its exact identity, unnecessary compute and provisioned storage could be removed through an authorized cleanup process.

The snapshot and required restoration metadata would remain for the agreed retention period.

If the customer returns, a new EBS data disk can be created from the retained snapshot and attached to a replacement Craxii machine.

The recovered machine must preserve the customer’s files, local SQLite state, and logical installation identity while re-establishing valid trusted worker credentials.

26.2 Retention Status

The one-year retention period is finalized. The final snapshot-based storage and restoration procedure remains subject to a focused technical and cost audit.

We have not finalized whether standard EBS snapshots or archived snapshots are preferable for this period.

The decision must account for snapshot storage costs, retrieval delays, restoration fees, and the expected frequency of customer reactivation.

We also need to distinguish between the retention of one final recoverable customer snapshot and the retention of ordinary daily backup history.

Customer-initiated deletion requests must be handled separately from ordinary cancellation. Where legally permissible, a valid deletion request should not be overridden merely because our standard product retention period is one year.

⸻

27. Backups and Recovery

Craxii V0.0.1 requires genuine backup and recovery capability.

The Universal Backend will maintain central RDS backups and separately coordinate customer data-disk snapshots.

27.1 Central Database Backups

Amazon RDS will maintain seven days of automated backups with point-in-time recovery.

We will verify that restoration works and take appropriate snapshots before potentially destructive database migrations.

27.2 Customer Machine Backups

The intended customer backup system uses daily encrypted EBS snapshots of persistent data disks.

The backend records snapshot ownership, source volume identity, completion status, and the associated installation.

Snapshots must represent recoverable customer state.

A raw snapshot taken while SQLite or other applications are actively writing may be crash-consistent without being fully application-consistent.

The implementation must therefore coordinate safe checkpoints, write quiescence, or another verified consistency mechanism.

The provisioning audit proposed seven daily and four weekly recovery points as an initial approach. That exact backup-retention schedule remains a recommendation rather than an explicitly approved policy.

27.3 Recovery

Automatic catastrophic machine replacement is deferred.

The initial system must nevertheless support safe operator-assisted recovery.

A failed machine should be recoverable by creating replacement compute, attaching the original persistent disk where appropriate, or restoring a snapshot onto a new disk.

The original disk must not be reformatted or destroyed merely because a replacement is being created.

Recovery must preserve customer ownership, worker identity fencing, and durable execution state.

Restore testing is mandatory before accepting paying customers.

⸻

28. Operational Reliability and Observability

The Universal Backend will initially be one logical service backed by PostgreSQL.

Important operations must survive application restarts.

Provisioning, wake requests, message delivery, payment reconciliation, backup scheduling, and other durable obligations must not depend solely on an in-memory Node.js process.

The current recommended approach is a PostgreSQL-backed background operation journal with bounded workers, database leases, unique constraints, and explicit reconciliation.

We do not need Kafka, Temporal, Step Functions, or another distributed workflow platform for the initial approximately 100-customer target.

28.1 Minimum Operational Visibility

We need to identify whether a customer’s subscription is active, whether their VM exists, which EBS volume belongs to them, whether the machine is running or sleeping, and whether the worker has authenticated and reported readiness.

We also need visibility into pending messages, provisioning failures, compute consumption, model spending, backup completion, and unresolved administrative operations.

Structured logs, essential metrics, and limited operator tooling are sufficient initially.

28.2 Essential Alerts

The initial system should alert on repeated provisioning failures, prolonged machine activation delays, missing backups, database availability problems, unexpected infrastructure spending, and significant model-budget anomalies.

A comprehensive monitoring dashboard is not required for V0.0.1.

28.3 Customer Activation Failures

A customer who successfully activates a trial or pays for a subscription must not lose their provisioning obligation because of a backend crash.

If activation takes longer than expected, the website should report the delay truthfully.

The earlier architecture established a 15-minute threshold for escalating delayed paid provisioning and directing customers to support rather than telling them to pay again.

The initial support contact is abhisht@craxii.com.

Normal provisioning retries should remain automatic where safe.

Ambiguous resource ownership, potential duplicate resources, or destructive recovery should instead require operator review.

⸻

29. Security Model and Trust Boundaries

Craxii executes model-generated instructions on computers containing persistent customer data.

Therefore, the model-controlled execution environment must be treated as potentially hostile.

Our security architecture separates customer-facing authority, central administrative authority, trusted worker authority, and model-controlled execution.

Cognito establishes customer identity.

The Universal Backend establishes customer ownership, subscription entitlement, and access rights.

AWS IAM restricts the backend’s cloud management permissions.

Worker cryptographic identities establish which machine is communicating with the backend.

The trusted Rust service maintains its protected machine credentials.

The model-controlled shell operates under a separate, restricted Unix identity.

29.1 Trusted Worker and Model-Controlled Shell

The existing Stage 27 Unix-user separation remains the foundation.

The trusted worker service has access to the resources necessary for authenticated communication and durable runtime management.

The model-controlled shell runs with lower privileges and must not read protected worker keys, AWS instance-profile credentials, central secrets, or other customers’ information.

Docker is not assumed to replace this operating-system privilege boundary.

29.2 Cloud Permissions

The Universal Backend will use a restricted IAM instance role.

Human operational permissions, release deployment permissions, machine-image publication permissions, and destructive cleanup authority should remain separate where appropriate.

A compromised control-plane process must not automatically gain unrestricted authority over the entire AWS account.

29.3 Resource Ownership

Every customer VM, EBS volume, and snapshot must be associated with an exact canonical installation identity.

AWS resource tags help with inventory, reconciliation, and policy restrictions, but tags alone do not prove that a resource belongs to the correct customer.

The backend must verify exact resource identifiers against PostgreSQL ownership records before performing sensitive lifecycle operations.

29.4 Encryption

Central database communication uses TLS.

Worker communication uses HTTPS with server-certificate verification.

Worker identities use cryptographic proof of possession.

RDS and EBS storage are encrypted at rest.

Secrets are not stored in source control or machine images.

These measures reduce exposure but do not replace correct authorization and privilege separation.

30. Deployment and Release Compatibility

The Universal Backend and Rust worker are independently deployed.

The control plane will be packaged as a Docker image and released through a controlled GitHub Actions and ECR workflow.

Customer workers will use pinned Rust release artifacts incorporated into versioned AMIs.

Changes to the Universal Backend must not require rebuilding every customer machine unless the worker contract or required behavior genuinely changes.

Backward compatibility is especially important because sleeping workers may reconnect after prolonged inactivity.

Protocol changes should be additive wherever practical.

The backend must retain support for worker protocol versions that still exist on customer machines, until those workers have been safely updated or retired.

A compilation test against the latest worker source does not establish compatibility with previously deployed binaries.

Controlled owner-canary testing will precede important worker changes.

Sophisticated fleet-wide automatic updates and advanced rollout infrastructure are deferred beyond V0.0.1.

31. Infrastructure Economics

Craxii’s infrastructure economics are determined primarily by dedicated customer compute, persistent storage, backup usage, and model inference.

The central Universal Backend is comparatively inexpensive.

Our selected central instances are EC2 t3.small and RDS db.t4g.small.

Earlier indicative pricing placed their combined instance-compute cost at approximately $47 per month, excluding storage, networking, backups, monitoring, and other infrastructure charges.

These figures remain planning estimates rather than verified monthly bills.

These figures exclude persistent EBS storage, operating-system disks, snapshots, networking, and model inference.

We must measure actual infrastructure costs during testing and track unsubsidized costs separately from cloud-credit consumption.

31.1 Cloud Credit Strategy

AWS credits will support computing infrastructure.

Azure credits will support eligible Azure AI Foundry inference.

This separation reflects our updated decision to concentrate all customer VMs and central infrastructure on AWS while using Azure primarily as a source of model inference runway.

The credits reduce our initial cash requirements but do not alter the underlying long-term unit economics.

Our resource allowances must remain financially defensible after credits expire.

31.2 Trial Economics

Trial users consume real AWS infrastructure and model resources without providing payment details.

That makes trial eligibility and resource caps important.

We have chosen a seven-day trial with $5 model usage and 12 EC2 running hours to give customers meaningful exposure while limiting financial risk.

The limits should be measured against actual customer task completion rather than evaluated only as infrastructure expenses.

32. Infrastructure and Features Explicitly Deferred

Craxii V0.0.1 deliberately excludes infrastructure that is not necessary to deliver the first self-service paid product.

Azure customer VM provisioning and AWS/Azure alternating placement are no longer part of the architecture. All customer compute now runs on AWS.

We are not building automatic catastrophic VM replacement, advanced cross-region recovery, per-customer VPCs, elaborate fleet orchestration, or a comprehensive administrative dashboard.

We are not creating a separate standalone model gateway service. The initial authenticated model proxy will be part of the Universal Backend.

We are not implementing advanced model-selection optimization before benchmarking establishes a concrete need.

We are not rewriting the existing Rust agent, replacing its local SQLite database, or transferring its entire persistent history into central PostgreSQL.

We will defer sophisticated memory retrieval and other agent-intelligence improvements unless they are necessary for the baseline product experience.

The scope reductions do not apply to fundamental security, entitlement enforcement, durable work admission, correct resource ownership, backup capability, or safe machine persistence.

Those remain V0.0.1 requirements.

34. Final Architecture Statement

Craxii V0.0.1 is a fully self-service AI agent product in which every customer receives a dedicated, persistent AWS computer that can be controlled through Telegram.

The customer-facing website remains a static Next.js application on Cloudflare, with AWS Cognito providing authentication and Razorpay processing subscriptions. The website communicates with api.craxii.com, which routes through Cloudflare and Caddy to the Dockerized TypeScript/Node.js Universal Backend running on an AWS Mumbai EC2 t3.small.

The Universal Backend maintains its authoritative operational state in a private Amazon RDS PostgreSQL db.t4g.small database. It manages customer accounts, trials, subscriptions, Telegram identities, secure worker communication, durable message delivery, AWS provisioning, machine lifecycle, resource allowances, model spending, backup coordination, and essential operational recovery.

Every customer receives a dedicated AWS EC2 t3.large running the existing Rust Craxii agent, with a separate encrypted 80 GiB persistent EBS data disk. Customer files, workspaces, conversation history, execution records, and local SQLite state remain on this disk. Machines safely stop after 30 minutes of inactivity and automatically wake when needed.

Workers authenticate over HTTPS using individual Ed25519 identities, signed challenges, short-lived sessions, and centrally enforced lifecycle generations. The model-controlled shell remains separated from trusted credentials and administrative authority.

Model inference is provided through Microsoft Azure AI Foundry, using eligible Azure startup credits and a centrally controlled model API interface. The specific model will be selected through later benchmarking.

Craxii costs $49 per month, including up to 180 cumulative EC2 running hours and $20 of model API usage per billing month. Customers may first experience Craxii through a seven-day free trial with 12 EC2 running hours and $5 of model usage, without providing a payment method. Trials end when their duration or either resource allowance is exhausted. No automatic overage charges apply.

Normal onboarding and machine provisioning are fully automated. Fundamental security, persistence, message correctness, spending enforcement, and recoverability are required from the beginning. Exceptional administrative recovery and sophisticated fleet operations may initially remain manual.

The architecture uses three repositories: the public Rust craxii agent, the private TypeScript craxii-control-plane, and the existing craxii_website.

Our objective is to ship a real, secure, persistent, commercially sellable Craxii as quickly as possible, without building infrastructure that does not materially contribute to the first customer experience.
